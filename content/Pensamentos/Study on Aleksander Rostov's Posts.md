Study on how Alexander Rostov structures his instagram posts.

How he writes about the art and the process of creation, including practical parts about the art or thoughts and concepts that emerge from it.
This extracts more of the media, creating an engaging story for it.

All the art talked about here is available on https://www.artofrostov.com/ 

The idea of this study is being able to apply the process on my own art, in a way to extract more from myself and exercise writing lore about each piece or collection of pieces, what he calls "drops".

## Drops

The drops work in a way to loosely group a collection of pieces together, in a way making it a larger body of work, that can attract people to click on the individual posts to see what is it part of.

Akin to anthologies, there's a loose theme to the drops, but enough to just kinda guide the mind into wandering the works.
All of them have a small text describing the theme and the overall idea behind. This text also highlights some of what's to come, gives some hints into the pieces and what to expect. It can work as *priming* the viewer: By setting some groundwork on the text and name of the drop, you guide the viewer towards some expectations, which can be followed on completely subverted.

Naming the drops is relevant. The names are easy to understand and very on the nose, but come with a hint of romance languages to it. FANTASTIKA, PORTRAITURA and PHILOSOPHIA, words that can be understood in lots of languages.

The first one is FANTASTIKA and it consists of works of "fantasy", so anything from dragons to sci-fi goes. He even goes into anime on one of the pieces, which shows how even if loose, the concept can take you out of the comfort zone and make you explore a little bit.
On PORTRAITURA, Rostov dives into portraits, the theme and inspiration varies, the final image being a portrait is what matters to the drop.
PHILOSOPHIA is still not out, but goes into the mind, the process itself will be livestreamed, so, in a way, it's a deeper connection to the artist itself, and not the pieces, that make this drop cohesive.

Interestingly as well, all three drops are connected in a funnel-like structure. The first one being very ample, with fanarts from other media, works that remind me of Disco and a big overall arrangement in a way that almost any person can like something in this drop.
On Portraitura, we get closer to the artist, with some of the pieces even being commissioned directly, we get more into what Rostov loves to do, which is faces, and while the theme is still loose, the connection we have to faces is deeper.
The last one being livestreamed shows how now that the attention has been captured, he can delve deeper into the processes and more complex themes, that wouldn't catch a random person's attention without the overarching lore.

## Posts

There's an evolution in how the posts are presented. At first keeping the art itself as the main cover picture of the instagram carousel, with text and descriptions coming second but on the portraits drop, the cover already has a number, title and small abstract on it, marking not only the fact that there's more by numbering them but also the fact that the texts begin to take part of the spotlight.
It's also interesting to note that the comments reflect the fact that text engages people. Most of the comments are coming from people reacting to the story being told, or giving their own view on what it meant to them.

## Texts

The stories being told on each picture are brought to the front by the text.
Not overly descriptive and always still leaving something open to interpretation, the texts tend to follow an order of explanation similar to what a museum guide would do:
1. Describing what the title means
2. Describing the main theme and where the inspiration comes from
3. Zooming in on specific parts of the painting, highlighting techniques and how they reflect or rival the theme
4. Speedpaint, showing the process
5. Picture of printed media and call to action to the available prints on his website
Sometimes a screenshot of brushes, a reference picture or a discarded idea might sneak in to show more of the inspiration

Pictures tend to be repeated in a lot of the slides of the carousel, sometimes zoomed in or cut in different ways to focus on different areas, but I believe this also helps keeping the same picture in the viewer's mind as they read.

In some pictures, the story behind the picture is the focus. On this case, Rostov goes on about what was going on in his life when he painted that. It gives the viewer a closer contact with the artist and, while not as descriptive of the work itself, can give a lot of context which opens more ways of absorbing the art

## Visual Cohesion

Rostov is mostly known as the Art Director in Disco Elysium. The fact that all of the text on the instagram posts are white serif lettering on top of a black slightly transparent background box ties into Disco's thoughts in-game.
Especially the accents in using bold, itallics or even a color sometimes to highlight some words. The colors directly give it an emotion, you can almost hear one of Disco's thought saying it.

The consistency between posts makes it feel more intentional, even though you can see how the form evolved, starting with more random texts, into a black box and finally into the slightly transparent perfectly framed box.

## How to apply

The order of operations is interesting here. While FANTASTIKA is a collection of older works, which then had to be united and had written texts about in the context of the collection, PORTRAITURA seems to have the theme set out beforehand and the texts feel almost like they've been written as the art was developing.

In my case, the best approach would be to get some things I like, try to wrap them up in a theme name that is loose enough to allow some experimenting and then write while the work is being done. Since 3D tends to have a longer timeframe of creation, documenting thoughts and WIP shots throughout the process can be helpful.
3D also has a lot of behind the scenes and stuff that wouldn't be seen or understood, so I believe this way of presenting the art can also be engaging for people to fully grasp what goes into the creation of the piece.
